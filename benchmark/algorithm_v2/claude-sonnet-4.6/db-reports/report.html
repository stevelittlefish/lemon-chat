Not financially worthwhile within 10 years under central assumptions. A 5 kWh system has ~25-year simple payback; a 10 kWh system ~19 years. Neither meets a 10-year horizon through tariff arbitrage alone.

Switching to a time-of-use tariff (e.g. Octopus Go) without buying any hardware saves £100–£320/year at zero capital cost — the single most financially efficient action available. The household already has a smart meter and qualifies immediately.

How the Arbitrage Works

The financial case for any standalone battery rests entirely on the price spread between cheap overnight electricity and expensive daytime electricity. The battery charges at the off-peak rate and discharges to avoid drawing from the grid at the peak rate.

The Home Office Structural Disadvantage

⚠ Critical factor unique to this household The home office consumes 10–20 kWh/day during daytime working hours — exactly when the battery must be discharging. This load cannot be shifted to the overnight cheap window. On a TOU tariff without a battery, that daytime load costs ~26.11p/kWh rather than the current ~24.7p/kWh — an extra ~1.4p/kWh on ~80% of consumption, costing roughly £100/yr more. S13S15 A TOU tariff is only beneficial if a battery is present or the household can genuinely shift 15–20% of consumption overnight.

Important Nuances & Caveats

The 70% Displacement Efficiency Assumption

The central model assumes 70% of battery discharge usefully displaces peak-rate grid consumption. If the battery management system is well-programmed and daytime loads are predictable, displacement efficiency could reach 85–90%, improving annual savings by ~20–30%. Conversely, irregular consumption patterns or poor BMS configuration could push it below 70%, worsening the case.

The 330-Cycle Assumption

The model uses 330 cycles/year rather than 365, allowing ~35 days for partial cycles, grid issues, or tariff changes. Ember's analysis of utility-scale systems confirms fewer than one full cycle per day is realistic even for optimised systems. S26 This household's high daytime demand supports daily full discharge, but overnight charging may occasionally be incomplete.

Non-Financial Value of Backup Power

ℹ Home office income protection This analysis focuses on financial returns as instructed. However, for a household whose income depends on the home office remaining operational during grid outages, backup power has real economic value not captured in the payback model. This benefit is genuinely material but impossible to quantify without knowing the household's income dependency.

The EV Wildcard

Without an EV, Intelligent Octopus Go's cheapest rates (~5.5–8p/kWh S15S19) are not accessible. If an EV is purchased within the 10-year period, the economics change materially — the off-peak window extends to 6 hours, overnight consumption rises dramatically, and battery arbitrage value increases significantly. An EV purchase would likely justify a larger battery on energy management grounds alone.

What to Do Before Making Any Decision

  1. 1
    Download your half-hourly smart meter data via Octopus, n3rgy, or the DCC. Establish precisely how much consumption falls in the 00:30–05:30 window vs. daytime peak hours. This is the single most important input for any financial model.
  2. 2
    Use Octopus Energy's switching tool to model the actual bill impact of Octopus Go against your specific half-hourly profile before considering a battery. If the tariff switch alone saves £200+/year, that is the priority action. S13
  3. 3
    Obtain at least three installer quotes for both 5 kWh and 10 kWh systems. Specify: LFP chemistry, AC-coupled configuration, full installed cost including inverter, commissioning, and grid registration.
  4. 4
    Verify manufacturer viability and warranty terms. GivEnergy's 2026 administration S10 proves this is not theoretical. Confirm what recourse exists if the manufacturer ceases trading. Prefer AlphaESS or Tesla Powerwall for stronger market positions. S20
  5. 5
    Confirm BMS programmability. Verify the battery management system can be programmed to charge only during the off-peak window and discharge automatically to meet daytime load — without manual intervention.
  6. 6
    Get specific VPP scheme terms from Axle Energy or the relevant platform S22 before treating VPP income as a financial input. Do not assume £150/yr without confirming current enrolment terms.
  7. 7
    Factor in an EV timeline. If an EV purchase is likely within 2–3 years, the improved economics of Intelligent Go may change the battery calculus significantly. Consider deferring the battery decision until EV plans are clearer. S19

Conclusion

✗ Battery without solar: Not financially worthwhile within 10 years

For this household — 9,000 kWh/year, home office with high daytime demand, limited manual load-shifting, no solar, 10-year horizon — neither a 5 kWh (~25-yr payback) nor a 10 kWh (~19-yr payback) battery recovers its installation cost within the assessment period through tariff arbitrage alone. Even the most optimistic realistic scenario (10 kWh at £5,350 with reliable VPP income) achieves only ~14-year payback.

✓ TOU tariff switch: Financially worthwhile — act first

Switching to Octopus Go or a comparable time-of-use tariff costs nothing, is immediately reversible, and delivers a positive 10-year financial outcome of £1,000–£3,200 depending on how much consumption can be shifted overnight. This is the first and possibly only action required.

The Strongest Counter-Arguments

Battery costs are falling. Tariff spreads may widen. A battery installed now at 0% VAT positions the household to benefit from future solar, vehicle-to-grid tariffs, or VPP grid services. The backup power benefit for a home-office household has real non-financial value. But these are contingent future benefits, not current financial realities — and the evidence does not support treating them as the basis for a £6,500 capital investment today.
