# Updated assessment: home battery without solar in England

## Bottom line

For this household, **switching to a suitable time-of-use (ToU) tariff without buying a battery is the strongest financial option**. On the illustrative assumptions, it saves about **£198 per year** with no capital cost or battery risk.

A battery can charge from the grid overnight and discharge during expensive periods; solar panels are not technically required for this use [S44][S45]. However, the household’s unusually high **daytime** office demand weakens the economics. Overnight energy cannot serve daytime consumption unless the battery is sufficiently large, powerful and permitted by the tariff to discharge then. The manufacturer’s claimed battery-only savings should be treated cautiously because they are marketing estimates for an unspecified system and household, not evidence for this property [S44].

Neither size is likely to pay for itself against the best available ToU alternative at typical prices. A 5 kWh system is less risky than a 10 kWh system, but even it requires an unusually low installed price or a much larger and more durable tariff spread.

## Tariff and policy assumptions

The current flat tariff is assumed to be **24.7p/kWh**. A July 2026 Ofgem-capped average rate is reported by Octopus at 26.11p/kWh, while Octopus advertises six-hour overnight electricity at 8p/kWh; however, Intelligent Octopus Go is principally an EV tariff and usually requires a compatible vehicle and charger [S13][S21][S22]. Cheap overnight rates available to all standalone battery owners should not be assumed to persist [S24].

British Gas markets tariffs intended for overnight battery charging [S31]. EDF advertises indicative overnight rates around 6.49–6.99p/kWh, but the precise rates, peak periods, standing charges and eligibility must be confirmed for this property [S36]. Agile tariffs may provide greater spreads but expose the household to volatile half-hourly prices and require dependable automated control [S18].

Before choosing a tariff, obtain written confirmation that:

- a standalone, grid-charged battery is eligible;
- grid charging, discharge and export are permitted;
- the supplier will not restrict automated battery operation;
- all rates, hours, standing charges and cancellation terms are clear.

The Smart Export Guarantee is for eligible renewable generation, not a general battery subsidy; battery export may require separate certification and tariff compliance [S37]. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme does not support batteries [S39]. No battery grant is assumed.

Qualifying professionally supplied and installed energy-saving materials may have attracted 0% VAT on 14 July 2026, subject to HMRC conditions, with a scheduled change to 5% from 1 April 2027; separately purchased equipment may be standard-rated [S26]. Quotations should state VAT treatment.

## Base-case model

Assumptions:

- Annual use: **9,000 kWh**
- Demand: 60% daytime, 20% peak/evening, 20% overnight
- Flat rate: **24.7p/kWh**
- ToU rates: 9p overnight, 24.5p daytime/shoulder, 30p peak
- Standing charges: excluded and assumed equal
- 5 kWh battery: 4.5 kWh usable capacity
- 10 kWh battery: 9 kWh usable capacity
- Round-trip efficiency: 90%
- Battery power: assumed adequate, but must be verified
- Useful operation: approximately 300 days yearly, not a full cycle every day
- Annual delivered battery energy: approximately 1,200 kWh for 5 kWh and 1,470 kWh for 10 kWh
- Battery savings decline by 2% annually
- Installed prices: £3,200–£4,200 for 5 kWh and £4,600–£7,100 for 10 kWh; midpoint costs £3,700 and £5,850 [S12]
- No maintenance, financing or replacement within ten years

The cycling assumption allows for nights when some electricity is used directly, insufficient peak demand, power limits, control failures and unprofitable price differences. It does not assume a full daily cycle.

## Ten-year outcome

Using a 2% annual reduction in battery savings, the ten-year multiplier is approximately 9.15:

| Option | Ten-year saving versus flat tariff | Capital cost | Net ten-year outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Flat tariff | £0 | £0 | £0 |
| ToU only | £1,980 | £0 | **+£1,980** |
| ToU + 5 kWh battery | £4,176 | £3,700 midpoint | **+£476** |
| ToU + 10 kWh battery | £4,670 | £5,850 midpoint | **–£1,180** |

Compared specifically with ToU without a battery:

- 5 kWh: £240 × 9.15 − £3,700 = approximately **–£1,500**
- 10 kWh: £294 × 9.15 − £5,850 = approximately **–£3,160**

Simple battery payback is approximately **13–18 years** for 5 kWh and **16–24 years** for 10 kWh. Inverter or battery replacement, maintenance, software charges, financing, electrical work and warranty throughput limits would worsen the result. Backup normally requires additional hardware such as a gateway; it should not be assumed to be included [S47].

## Key sensitivities

Approximate annual battery savings at different effective spreads are:

| Effective spread | 5 kWh | 10 kWh |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10p/kWh | £107 | £131 |
| 20p/kWh | £240 | £294 |
| 30p/kWh | £347 | £425 |

At a sustained 30p spread, midpoint payback is still about 11 years for 5 kWh and 14 years for 10 kWh. At only 10% peak consumption, savings fall to roughly £120 and £180 annually; at 30% peak consumption they rise to approximately £240 and £360, subject to capacity and power limits.

At base utilisation, battery-only ten-year payback would require an installed price of about **£2,200 or less for 5 kWh** or **£2,700 or less for 10 kWh**, before replacement costs. Degradation of 3–4%, lower usable capacity, restricted power or an inverter replacement reduces those thresholds.

## Recommendation and decision checklist

1. **Switch tariff first.** A suitable ToU tariff could save about £200 annually without buying equipment, although the actual saving depends on how much existing consumption naturally occurs overnight.
2. **Prefer no battery financially.** It has the best expected ten-year outcome and no degradation, warranty or tariff-lock-in risk.
3. **Consider 5 kWh only** with a very low quotation, robust automated control, a durable spread and substantial measured evening/peak demand.
4. **Avoid 10 kWh solely because annual use is high.** The extra capacity is worthwhile only if interval data shows it can regularly discharge at high prices. BYD and GivEnergy offer modular or scalable systems, but the cited product pages do not establish the required economics, current technical specifications or achievable cycling for this use case [S46][S47].

The strongest argument against this recommendation is that future volatility and flexibility markets could make automated battery arbitrage much more valuable than this conservative model [S40][S41][S42][S43]. Conversely, tariffs may narrow, eligibility may change, software may fail, and replacement costs may rise.

Before purchase, obtain 12 months of half-hourly smart-meter data, actual flat and ToU quotations including standing charges, and at least three itemised battery quotations. Confirm usable capacity, continuous and peak charge/discharge power, measured round-trip efficiency, degradation and capacity-retention guarantees, throughput limits, reserve settings, backup hardware and outage performance, monitoring or software fees, VAT, DNO requirements, maintenance and replacement pricing. Specifically measure how much office demand falls within each tariff’s peak period.
