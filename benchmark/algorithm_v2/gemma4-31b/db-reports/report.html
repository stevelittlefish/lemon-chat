Is a home battery worthwhile without solar panels?

Financial analysis for a UK household (9,000 kWh/yr) with a high-demand home office. Report Date: July 2026

Executive Summary

For a UK household, a standalone battery is a financial tool for "tariff arbitrage"—charging during cheap overnight windows and discharging during expensive peaks. While a 5 kWh system is likely a net loss or marginal gain over 10 years, a 10 kWh system is financially viable, potentially offering a net gain of £2,700 over a decade.

A critical catalyst is the 0% VAT incentive for standalone retrofits, available until March/April 2027 [S10] [S12].

1. The Concept: Tariff Arbitrage

Without solar panels to generate "free" energy, the battery acts as a financial buffer. By switching to a Time-of-Use (TOU) tariff, the homeowner can buy electricity at low rates (7p–12p/kWh) and avoid paying high peak rates (up to 45p/kWh) [S1] [S6].

3. Deep Dive: The Options

Safe

Flat-Rate Tariff

The "do nothing" approach. No financial risk, but no optimization. Guaranteed expenditure of £22,000+ over 10 years.

Risky

TOU (No Battery)

Generally a poor strategy. Higher peak rates (30p-45p) would likely outweigh manual shifting, increasing the total bill [S6] [S7].

Marginal

5 kWh Battery + TOU

Usable arbitrage is ~4.5 kWh/day. Payback is nearly a decade. Only suitable if backup power is prioritized over ROI [S15].

Recommended

10 kWh Battery + TOU

The most compelling option. Covers a significant portion of the 10-20 kWh daily office demand. Net gain of ~£2,700 over 10 years [S17].

4. Technical & Market Realities

Hardware Factors

  • Efficiency: Modern LFP batteries operate at 90-98% round-trip efficiency [S9].
  • Usable Capacity: Depth of Discharge (DoD) means a 10 kWh battery may only provide 9 kWh of usable energy [S12].
  • Lifespan: LFP batteries typically last 10-15 years, though capacity degrades 2-3% annually [S9].

Market Risks

The biggest risk is Tariff Volatility. If the spread between off-peak and peak prices narrows (e.g., from 25p to 15p), annual savings drop significantly, and the payback period may extend beyond 10 years [S1] [S7].
