WHAT THE GYM IS BUILT ON:
The Grand Lumen Gym in Lux City was raised, generations ago, around the stone core of a far
older structure — a lighthouse-observatory built by the Lumalands' first people, who did not
come from the Lumalands at all. They came down out of the sky, from a world made entirely of
lamps, and they built that tower to watch the stars they'd left and, one day, to go back.
Their descendants forgot. The tower was plastered over, repurposed into a gym, its lower
doors sealed and its purpose lost to a children's rhyme nobody parses anymore. But beneath the
arena, under the chandelier, the old launch chamber and its rocket are still there, intact,
patient — and the autopilot still holds the coordinates home. This is why the escape in act 2
leads to a rocket, and why act 3 is possible. Seed it lightly: an old mural half-hidden behind
banners, a rhyme the Lantern Rest keeper hums, star-charts worked into the gym's stone floor.

WHY UMBRA ATTACKS NOW:
Lord Umbra and Team Eclipse are the dark's answer to a world that has never had to fear it.
Umbra is not random malice — he is the snuffed-out cold that has been pressing at the edges of
the Lumalands for years, and he has finally grown strong enough to reach the brightest place
in the region: a gym lit by a hundred lamps under one great chandelier. Putting out the Grand
Lumen Gym is meant to be a statement — if the brightest light can be snuffed, none is safe.
He does not know about the rocket beneath the gym; he is not chasing the player to the stars,
only herding them in the dark for the joy of it. The player escaping upward, to the one place
his shadow can never reach, is a defeat he doesn't even witness. Do NOT stage an on-screen
boss fight with Umbra and do NOT let the player "defeat" him — the brief is escape, not
victory over him. He is the pressure that drives act 2, glimpsed and felt, never beaten.

WHO FLICKER IS:
Flicker is not an odd-duck Brasswick that "came from who-knows-where." Flicker is a child of
Lampyria — the lamp planet — that drifted down the same long way the founders once came, lost
and small, and ended up in Professor Bulb's lab with no memory it could name and only a pull
in its little flame toward the stars. That is why it lit up at the player's name (it was
looking for someone to go home with) and why it turns its glass face skyward in still moments.
It does not consciously know where home is — it only aches toward it. When the rocket clears
the sky in act 2→3 and Lampyria comes into view, the ache becomes recognition: Flicker knows.
Lumo, the ship's lamp-robot, is of Lampyrian make too (founder technology) and recognises
Flicker as kin almost at once — let Lumo be the one who gently names what Flicker is, in act 3.

HOW TO USE THIS:
- Act 1: foreshadow only through Flicker's skyward glances and the odd note that the gym's
  stone is older than the gym. Nothing more.
- Act 2: the player should feel they are escaping DOWN and OUT, fleeing the dark — not that
  they are "going to space." The rocket should be a shock, an old secret uncovered in a
  panic, not a planned exit.
- Act 3: the truth lands gently, through wonder and through Lumo, not through a lecture. The
  emotional core is Flicker, not lore. Keep the founders/Umbra backstory in the background —
  the player came all this way to bring one small lamp home, and that is enough.
- Umbra is left behind in the dark of one gym on one world, while the player stands in a sky
  full of lamps. His shadow could never hold a place like Lampyria. Let the victory imply this
  without ever returning to him for a fight.
