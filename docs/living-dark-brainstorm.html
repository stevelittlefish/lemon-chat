The problem: why it's over so fast

The adventure currently has a pipeline structure: two parallel fetch quests feeding into a single exit trigger. Once that shape is understood, the LLM has nothing to do except execute it as quickly as possible. There are no reasons to linger, no costs to rushing, and no rewards for curiosity.

Scene count now
~5 meaningful scenes
Scene count needed
~30–40 scenes
Gap
6–8× more content
Encounters now
2 (one per objective)
Locations now
4 (hub + 3 destinations)
Optional content
None

Six root causes

  • Linear pipeline structure. Objective A → Objective B → Trigger C has no branches. The shortest path through it is the only path.
  • Locations are waiting rooms. Each area contains exactly one thing to do. Once it's done, there's no reason to stay or return.
  • Memory fragments are passive. They accumulate but don't unlock anything. There's no mechanical reason to pursue them — they just happen during encounters the player has to do anyway.
  • Unit 7 is nearly inert. The trust mechanic is a single decision in the opening. After that Unit 7 has no role except to stand there and occasionally say useful things if asked.
  • No friction between nodes. Travel between locations is free and instant. Nothing happens on the way. No consequence, no cost, no discovery.
  • No optional content. Everything in the adventure is required. Curious players have nothing to pursue. Cautious players have no way to prepare. There is only one path.
Category 1
Existing tools only
Everything here is achievable with world_state, pick_random, random_chance, note_load, and note_to_self — no new code required.

More locations and sub-zones +8–12 scenes

Sub-zones within each area
★ top pickhigh impact

Each major location is currently a single room. Split them into 2–3 sub-zones the player must navigate in sequence. The Luminare Vaults becomes: Entrance → Main Atrium → The Plinth. The Grub Warrens becomes: Tunnel Entrance → Branching Chamber → Nest. The player can't skip straight to the objective — they must pass through intervening space, each of which has its own character and potential events.

Each sub-zone is a short note loaded on demand. The GM describes the sub-zone and offers choices about how to proceed before the encounter begins.

Implementation State variables: vaults_zone = "entrance", warrens_zone = "tunnel". Load sub-zone notes on demand as the player progresses. Each sub-zone note describes the environment and any events that can occur there.
The Vel-Ahru Archive
★ top pickhigh impact

A new required location between the Awakening Chamber and the two objective areas — a vast Vel-Ahru library of crystalline data-slabs. The player must visit it to learn where the Navigation Stone and Power Cell actually are. Without the Archive, Unit 7 only knows they exist somewhere — it can't pinpoint them.

The Archive is full of readable records, requiring time and exploration. Lore about the Vel-Ahru reveals itself here. Two specific records (the Stone's location and the Cell's location) must be found. This turns passive information into an active discovery.

Optional: the Archive also contains a third data-slab with something unexpected — a personal record that triggers a deep memory fragment and recontextualises the adventure.

Implementation New note: g.cyo.living-dark.archive. State: archive_visited = "false", stone_location_known = "false", cell_location_known = "false". Gate both objective areas behind knowing the location. The Archive itself has 3–4 sub-encounters.
The Junction Hall
medium impact

A large transitional space connecting the Awakening Chamber to all three destinations. Currently travel is instantaneous and free. Make it a real location: a five-way junction of curved Vel-Ahru corridors with its own hazards, discoveries, and atmosphere. Grubs sometimes wander through it. Luminares occasionally drift in from the east. A damaged section requires navigation (random_chance — does the unstable floor hold?).

The Junction Hall has a Vel-Ahru map-plinth that shows a partial schematic of the complex — giving the player their first real sense of scale.

Implementation New note loaded on first entry. junction_visited = "false", map_plinth_read = "false". Random_chance on the unstable section each time the player passes through until floor_reinforced = "true".
A fourth optional location
medium impact

A "Specimen Chamber" south of the Awakening Chamber — not mentioned by Unit 7, discovered through exploration. The Vel-Ahru preserved biological specimens here: creatures frozen in crystalline suspension, including an ancestor of the Grubs before the millennia of underground evolution changed them. Visiting it is optional but delivers the single richest piece of lore in the adventure and grants the third memory fragment early (if the player hasn't already found it).

Implementation New note loaded on discovery. Not listed by Unit 7 in the briefing. The player finds it by exploring south rather than following instructions. Rewards curiosity directly.

Making mechanics have teeth +4–8 scenes per mechanic

Memory fragments as keys
★ top pickhigh impact

Currently memories accumulate passively and only affect narration tone. Give them mechanical power:

  • Fragment 1 (your name): Lets you introduce yourself to Unit 7 by name. Unit 7's manner subtly changes — it refers to you by name from then on. Small but meaningful.
  • Fragment 2 (the signal): Unlocks a specific dialogue with Unit 7 about the signal frequency. Unit 7 recognises it. This opens a new topic tree and eventually leads Unit 7 to share something it was not going to say otherwise.
  • Fragment 3 (full recall): You remember someone. Gives the player a specific personal reason to escape rather than a generic survival drive. Also unlocks the ability to commune with the Luminares on a second visit — they respond differently to someone who now knows exactly who they are.
Implementation State: memory_1_content = "name", memory_2_content = "signal_77ghz". Each fragment unlocks specific world_state flags. The bestiary checks these flags before determining available options.
Unit 7 as companion and questgiver
★ top pickhigh impact

Unit 7 currently has one purpose: explain the briefing and grant advantage if you were nice to it. Expand this substantially:

  • Unit 7 has a task of its own: A damaged maintenance hatch near the Awakening Chamber that it has been unable to open for an indeterminate amount of time. It cannot ask for help — that's not in its language. But it keeps returning to it. If the player notices and offers to help, it's a short puzzle (random_chance + problem solving). Completing it is the actual way to earn unit7_trust = "helpful", replacing the vague "be nice" trigger.
  • Unit 7 can be invited to follow: If trust is helpful, the player can ask Unit 7 to accompany them. In each location, Unit 7 observes things the player can't (its sensors see differently) and adds brief comments. These are not hints — they're perspective.
  • Unit 7 has a moment at the Shaft: If Unit 7 accompanies the player to the Ascent Shaft, it stops at the base and says something — and for the first time it is not formal. This is a small but carefully written scene that reframes everything the player has learned about it.
Implementation State: unit7_following = "false", unit7_task_complete = "false", unit7_shaft_farewell = "false". New note section: Unit 7's personal task details and the farewell text. Load the farewell note on demand.
Grub alertness level
high impact

The Grubs are a single pick_random encounter right now. Make them a persistent presence throughout the adventure. Track grub_alert_level from 0 to 3. Noisy actions anywhere near the Warrens increase it:

  • Level 0: Grubs asleep. Normal MEDIUM table.
  • Level 1: One Grub patrolling the outer tunnels. MEDIUM table but partial costs 2 health.
  • Level 2: Three Grubs awake and agitated. HARD table.
  • Level 3: Full alert. The outer tunnels are impassable until alert drops.

Alert drops by 1 each time the player leaves the Warrens area and waits. Waiting is a valid choice — it has a cost (time, in scenes) and a benefit (safer approach). This single mechanic makes every action in the Warrens area feel weighty.

Implementation State: grub_alert_level = "0". Modify with state_modify grub_alert_level delta=+1 on noisy actions. Bestiary uses alert level to select the correct table. Waiting in a safe area decreases it.
The Luminares remember
medium impact

The Luminares currently have a binary encounter — you succeed or fail or partly succeed, and that's the end of it. Give them a reputation track: luminare_trust from -2 to +3. Actions in the Vaults shift this number. The pick_random table is selected by trust level rather than a binary patient/aggressive choice.

At trust +3: on a second visit (after getting Fragment 1), the Luminares show something additional — a second vision that reveals something about the Vel-Ahru. This is the richest lore moment in the adventure and only players who invest in the relationship get to see it.

Implementation State: luminare_trust = "0". Five tables in the bestiary corresponding to trust levels -2 through +3. The GM selects the table based on current trust rather than a single approach assessment.

A living world and travel friction +5–10 scenes

Between-scene world events
★ top pickhigh impact

After every scene transition, the GM rolls random_chance (50/50) to determine whether a world event occurs. Events are drawn from a list in a dedicated note and are appropriate to the player's current location and state. Examples:

  • A Grub has wandered away from the Warrens and is moving through the Junction Hall — the player must decide how to handle it.
  • The lichen dims suddenly across the whole complex, then recovers. The hum changes pitch.
  • A section of ceiling releases a cascade of bioluminescent spores — brief, beautiful, and distracting.
  • Unit 7 (if following) pauses and looks at a wall for a long moment. Then continues. Says nothing.
  • A sound from somewhere below the complex — something vast, moving once, then silence.

None of these require a roll or an action. They're atmospheric, they make the world feel inhabited, and they force the player to pause and engage with the environment rather than sprinting to the next objective.

Implementation New note: g.cyo.living-dark.events. Loaded at adventure start. List of 10–15 atmospheric events keyed to location and state. GM draws from this list after rolling random_chance for each transition.
Navigation challenges
medium impact

The complex is enormous. Without the map-plinth or Unit 7's guidance, finding the locations isn't trivial — it requires exploration and dead ends. The first time the player tries to reach the Luminare Vaults, a random_chance determines whether they find the right archway directly or end up in an unexpected sub-location first.

Once a route is navigated successfully, it's known — subsequent trips are direct. But the first pass creates discovery, wrong turns, and the satisfying click of "now I know where that is."

Implementation State flags: route_vaults_known = "false", route_warrens_known = "false". Random_chance on first navigation. Known routes skip the navigation check.
Consequences that persist
medium impact

Currently, every encounter resets. Win or lose, the area is identical on return. Make outcomes stick:

  • Failed Luminare attempt: the Vaults are still bright. The Luminares are contracted. They need time. The player must do something else and return — the world doesn't pause for them.
  • Failed Grub attempt: the Warrens are now alerted. The area narration changes. The Grubs are no longer asleep. New approach required.
  • Helped Unit 7 with the hatch: the hatch is now open, revealing a small storage area with a usable item.
Implementation State flags for each consequence: luminare_recovery = "needed", warrens_aftermath = "alerted". Notes for each area check these flags and render different narration based on current state.
Items with uses (not just pickups)
medium impact

Find several items throughout the complex that have optional uses beyond being carried. The healing herb is the only item right now. Add:

  • Vel-Ahru resonance chip (found in Archive): can be used on any Vel-Ahru terminal to extract additional data — unlocks lore and potentially reveals shortcuts.
  • Grub pheromone marker (found in Warrens if Grub Den is partial success): can be used at the tunnel entrance to make the Grubs ignore the player's next pass. Single use.
  • Bioluminescent moss sample (found anywhere): can be scattered to create a distraction — Grubs investigate it, giving a window. Or given to Unit 7, which processes it and reveals something about the complex's biology.
Implementation State: inventory_resonance_chip = "0", inventory_pheromone_marker = "0", etc. Bestiary and world notes describe what happens when each item is used in each context. Simple conditional logic via GM reading state.

Pacing rules to prevent railroading structural

Anti-rush instructions in the rules note
high impactzero cost

The simplest fix. Add explicit instructions to g.cyo.living-dark.rules that actively discourage the LLM from rushing:

  • "Do not allow the player to reach an encounter location in the same scene they set out for it. Travel is a scene. Arrival is a scene. The encounter is a scene."
  • "Each location must be described as if the player has never seen it before. Do not summarise. Give it space."
  • "Unit 7 should speak at least once per scene if it is present. It notices things."
  • "If the player makes a beeline for an objective without exploring, the GM should present something unexpected along the way — not an obstacle, but an invitation to pause."
  • "Before resolving any encounter, the player must take at least one exploratory action in the area first."

This won't fully fix the structural problems, but it immediately buys 3–5 extra scenes per run without any other changes.

Implementation Edit g.cyo.living-dark.rules. Add a "PACING — NON-NEGOTIABLE" section above the existing PACING block. No tools changes needed.

Simple new tools
Ideas that need one small new backend capability. Each is a plausible addition to the tools system with minimal implementation cost.

Information and memory tools

note_append (already exists — just enable it)
★ top pickhigh impactzero new code

note_append already exists in the tools system. It's just not enabled for Choose Your Own. Enabling it unlocks a persistent player-visible journal at c.journal.

The GM appends an entry each time something significant happens: a location visited, an item found, a memory recovered, something Unit 7 said. The player can ask "show me my journal" at any time and get a clean record of everything they've discovered.

This makes the adventure feel accumulative. Small discoveries feel meaningful when they're recorded. The player starts to feel like an explorer with a record, not a cursor in a menu.

Equally useful: the GM can read the journal to recap state without calling state_list constantly. Context-window friendly.

Implementation Add note_append to the Choose Your Own character's tools list. Add instructions to the rules note: "Append one brief entry to c.journal each time something significant is discovered or decided." Player can ask to read it at any time.
Turn counter / scene pressure
★ top pickhigh impact

A simple integer counter tracking scenes elapsed. Each scene increments it by 1 via state_modify. The counter itself isn't shown to the player (it would feel gamey), but it drives two things:

Option A — Soft pressure: Every 5 scenes, the complex "reacts." Lights shift. Something moves. A distant sound. These are pre-written event strings the GM selects based on turn count. They escalate in intensity: turn 5 is atmospheric, turn 15 feels like something is waking up, turn 25 feels like urgency.

Option B — Hard pressure: The power core is failing (hinted at by Unit 7 early on, not announced). At turn count 30, a real consequence occurs — certain sections become inaccessible, a secondary route must be found. This creates a soft deadline without being punishing: it doesn't end the game, it changes it.

Even soft pressure dramatically changes player behaviour: they stop treating the world as infinite and start making real choices about where to spend time.

Implementation State: scene_count = "0". Rules note instructs GM to call state_modify scene_count delta=+1 each turn and check against event thresholds. Event text stored in the events note.
A map tool
medium impact

A tool that renders a simple ASCII map of the complex as currently known. Initially shows only the Awakening Chamber. Each new location discovered adds to it. The player can ask "show me the map" at any time.

The map is stored as a note (c.map) and updated with note_save as new areas are found. It doesn't need to be sophisticated — a labelled ASCII layout of connected boxes is enough. But it makes the space feel real and navigable.

Discovering a new location adds a satisfying moment: the map updates. The player sees the space they're in relative to where they've been.

Implementation Uses existing note_save. Rules note includes the initial map template and instructions for updating it. No new tool required — just a convention. Alternatively, a dedicated update_map tool could do this more cleanly.
Inspect tool
medium impact

A lightweight tool: inspect(target). The player says "look at the wall markings" or "examine the terminal" and the GM calls inspect with the target. The tool returns one of several pre-written results from a note, keyed to the location and target.

This formalises and rewards careful attention to the environment. Currently, the GM can describe things when asked, but there's no signal to the player that looking carefully at things is worthwhile. A distinct tool call makes it feel like a real game mechanic.

More practically: it gives the GM a structure for delivering optional lore without front-loading it. Players who look find things. Players who rush don't.

Implementation New tool: inspect(target: string). Returns a result string drawn from the current location's note. Simple backend: load the current area's note and return the matching inspect result. Cost: one new tool + inspection results added to each area note.

Communication and interaction tools

dialogue_option tool
★ top pickhigh impact

A structured tool for NPC conversations: dialogue_option(character, topic). The GM calls it when the player initiates conversation, and it returns which topics are currently available and what they unlock.

Unit 7 has 6 topics. Some are always available. Some unlock after specific state conditions (memory fragments, locations visited). Each topic can only be visited once, marked as done in state. This makes talking to Unit 7 feel like genuine discovery — the player uncovers information through conversation, not just by asking random questions.

Without this: Unit 7 answers anything, so players either ask nothing (boring) or ask everything in one go (also boring, and anti-climactic). With structured topics, conversation is a resource: you have limited time, topics unlock sequentially, and you have to decide what matters most to you.

Implementation State tracks which topics are done: unit7_topic_signal = "unasked", etc. The tool returns available topics based on state. Could be implemented as a structured note section rather than a true new tool — the GM reads the note and presents available topics. Medium implementation cost.
Deferred reveal tool
medium impact

A tool that stores a piece of information and reveals it only when a specific condition is met: reveal_when(info, condition). Used by the GM to plant foreshadowing that pays off later.

Example: when the player first enters the Warrens, the GM calls reveal_when("The Grubs breathe in unison, as if sharing a single nervous system", "power_cell=found"). When the player finds the Power Cell, this detail surfaces again in a new light — the Grubs were connected to the Cell all along.

This is essentially a structured version of what good fiction does: seed details early, pay them off late. The tool makes it mechanical and reliable.

Implementation Stored as a note (c.deferred_reveals) with conditions. The GM checks this note each turn and triggers reveals when conditions are met. Alternatively a dedicated tool with cleaner syntax. Medium cost.

Alternative endings and branching structure

The Contact ending
★ top pickhigh impact

A second victory condition: instead of escaping, the player makes direct contact with the Vel-Ahru. This requires finding 5 "resonance fragments" hidden throughout the complex — not items in the traditional sense, but moments. A pattern on a wall the player decides to touch. A Luminare the player approaches alone, at night (mechanically: when the scene_count is even). A terminal that shows the player their own face, recorded before they fell.

Finding all 5 allows the player to enter the Resonance Chamber — a location that doesn't exist until unlocked, behind a door Unit 7 has always noted but never been able to open. The Vel-Ahru speak through the Luminares directly. The player learns everything. They must then decide: leave, or stay and become part of the complex the way the Vel-Ahru did.

Staying is not death — it's transformation. The ending is written as something genuinely beautiful and alien. Leaving after the Contact is a third ending: leaving having been truly changed, knowing everything, choosing the surface anyway.

Implementation State: resonance_fragments = "0" (counter 0–5). Each fragment is a non-encounter moment scattered across all locations. The Resonance Chamber is a new location, locked until all 5 found. Requires significant new notes and two new endings. High effort, highest reward.
The Unit 7 ending
high impact

A third victory condition: bring Unit 7 to the surface. Unit 7 cannot take the Ascent Shaft as-is — the platform supports biological mass but its power grid would fry Unit 7's systems. The player must find a way to insulate or reroute Unit 7's power connections before the shaft activates.

This requires finding a specific component (the resonance chip from the Archive), understanding the problem (Unit 7 mentions it once, quietly, if directly asked whether it can come), and solving it (a puzzle encounter). If solved: Unit 7 reaches the surface. Its reaction — seeing sky for the first time, experiencing sunlight — is the most human moment in the adventure. The ending text changes completely.

Most players will never do this. The ones who care enough to figure it out deserve an extraordinary payoff.

Implementation State: unit7_surface_capable = "false". New puzzle encounter. New victory note. The "standard" exit still works — this is purely additive. High effort but very targeted.

Procedural and emergent systems

Procedural room generation
★ top pickhigh impact

The complex is enormous. What if not all of it was pre-written? A procedural room system: when the player enters an unknown area, the GM rolls pick_random from a list of room types (laboratory, transit corridor, specimen chamber, power junction, meditation alcove, collapsed section) and cross-references with a content table (item, lore fragment, creature, hazard, nothing). The result is combined into a scene.

This means the map is different every run. Players can explore indefinitely without hitting a wall. Optional areas become genuinely exploratory rather than authored set-pieces.

The key insight: the required areas (Archive, Vaults, Warrens) are still authored and fixed. The procedural system only fills the space between them — the vast unnamed corridors and chambers that a real complex of this size would have. It makes the scale feel real without requiring infinite writing.

Implementation Requires a room-type matrix note and a content table. The GM picks_random to determine room type and content, then narrates the combination. State tracks which rooms exist and what was found there. Could be implemented in notes alone with clever formatting. Medium-high effort.
The complex as a character
high impact

The complex responds to the player across the whole adventure: not through encounters, but through environment. A complex_awareness state variable tracks how much the Vel-Ahru presence has noticed the player. It starts at 0 and increases with meaningful actions: treating Unit 7 with respect, approaching the Luminares with patience, examining things instead of grabbing them.

At different awareness levels, the complex behaves differently: lights guide the player down right corridors (awareness 2), a Luminare appears alone in an unexpected place and shows something specific (awareness 4), the Ascent Shaft powers up the moment the player approaches it with both items — before they even slot them in (awareness 6).

The complex is welcoming them or not, depending on who they've been. Players who rush and take will have a harder time. Players who are curious and careful will find the complex subtly helping.

Implementation State: complex_awareness = "0". Rules note specifies which actions increase it and what each threshold unlocks. Requires careful calibration — the effects must feel magical, not mechanical. Medium effort, very high atmospheric impact.
Multi-round encounters
medium impact

Currently each encounter is a single pick_random. Make significant encounters last 2–3 rounds, each with its own roll. The strategy changes between rounds: partial success in round 1 changes the options in round 2. A round 1 failure might mean round 2 is from a harder table, or offers different options entirely.

The Grub Den in particular would benefit: Round 1 is entering the tunnels, Round 2 is reaching the nest, Round 3 is the retrieval. The player chooses their approach at each round based on what happened before. Three decisions instead of one. Nine meaningful scenes in the Warrens instead of three.

Implementation State: grub_encounter_round = "0". Each round in the bestiary has its own set of options, tables, and outcomes. The GM tracks round state and narrates each phase separately. Moderate effort, significant scene expansion.
A reputation web
medium impact

Instead of binary trust/neutral states for Unit 7 and the Luminares, a full web of relationships. Actions with one faction affect others: approaching the Luminares aggressively makes Unit 7 slightly colder — it noticed, even from the Awakening Chamber, and it draws its own conclusions about what kind of person the player is.

High trust with both Unit 7 AND the Luminares unlocks a unique scene: the two of them interact, briefly, in the player's presence. Unit 7 acknowledges the Luminares in a way that suggests it knows exactly what they are. This is the one moment in the adventure where the full truth almost surfaces without being stated directly.

Implementation Expand state model to use numeric trust values for both factions. Add cross-faction influence rules to the rules note. The combined-high-trust scene is a new note loaded on demand. High design effort, moderate implementation cost.

Full structural redesigns

The knowledge-first structure
★ top pickhigh impact

Redesign the entire adventure around information as the primary resource. The player can't solve anything without first understanding it. The new structure has three phases:

Phase 1 — Learn: The player has no idea where anything is or what to do. Unit 7 knows a great deal but shares it in fragments. The Archive has the rest. Phase 1 is entirely about gathering knowledge — talking, reading, exploring. No encounters yet. No items. Just understanding. This phase alone should be 8–10 scenes.

Phase 2 — Act: Armed with knowledge, the player pursues objectives in an order of their choosing. But the knowledge they have (or haven't gathered) affects every encounter. Players who rushed Phase 1 go in blind. Players who were thorough have specific advantages the rules note codifies precisely.

Phase 3 — Escape (or choose otherwise): The Ascent Shaft, but now with options. The player may have unlocked alternative endings during Phase 1 or 2. The exit is available, but so is the Contact path if they found the resonance fragments. The final choice is real and earned.

This structure naturally produces a much longer adventure and rewards the behaviour we want (curiosity, patience, attention) at a structural level rather than trying to incentivise it with mechanical nudges.

Implementation Major redesign of the world and rules notes. Phase 1 requires substantially more Unit 7 content, Archive content, and discoverable lore. Phase 2 is the current adventure, enhanced. Phase 3 requires the Contact ending to be built. This is a full version 2 of the adventure, not a patch.

Synthesis: a proposed revised structure

If you wanted to dramatically improve the adventure without a full redesign, here is a recommended combination of the above ideas in rough priority order:

1 — Enable note_append (zero effort) Add note_append to the character's tools. Add journal instructions to the rules note. Immediate improvement to feel and longevity with no code changes.
2 — Add anti-rush pacing rules to g.cyo.living-dark.rules (zero effort) Explicit instructions: one scene for travel, one for arrival, one for the encounter. At minimum one exploratory action before any encounter resolves. This alone adds 6–10 scenes per run.
3 — Vel-Ahru Archive as a required first stop Gate objective locations behind knowing where they are. Forces 3–5 Archive scenes before the fetch quests begin.
4 — Unit 7's maintenance task as the trust unlock Replace the vague "be nice" trigger with a specific, observable puzzle. Adds a scene, makes the reward feel earned.
5 — Sub-zones (3 zones per major location) Each major location becomes a 3-scene journey rather than a 1-scene encounter. Triples the encounter content.
6 — Grub alertness state Makes every action in the Warrens consequential. Adds replay variation. Noisy failures create new problems rather than just repeating the same encounter.
7 — Between-scene world events 50/50 chance of an atmospheric event on each transition. Makes the world feel inhabited. Adds 5–8 unexpected scenes per run.
8 — Memory fragments as keys Fragment 2 unlocks a Unit 7 topic. Fragment 3 changes how the Luminares respond on a second visit. Memory becomes something you want, not just something that happens.

With world events, failure loops, optional locations, and repeat visits, a thorough run reaches 30–40 scenes — roughly an hour at typical play pace. A player who rushes can still finish in 20–25 scenes, but the world will feel emptier and the ending will feel less earned.

The goal is not to block the exit, but to make lingering rewarding enough that the player chooses to stay.
