The homecoming flame stands tall and gold in the heart of the Brass Hollow, and around it the
brass oil-lamps are all alight — and brightest among them, dancing from hand to hand and
flame to flame, is the little dented lamp you carried all the way from a windmill town on a
green world half a sky away.

Flicker.

It came to Professor Bulb's lab from who-knows-where, and never quite settled, and lit up the
day it heard your name — because some part of it knew that you were the one who would take it
the long way round to here. Every still evening it turned its small glass face up at the
stars, and now you know what it was looking for. It found it. You brought it.

For a moment you feel the size of the goodbye coming, and it is large. But Flicker hops back
to you across the warm stones and bumps its side against your hand, the way it always has when
you were uncertain — and behind it the whole brass family comes forward, glowing, and the
eldest of them, a great soft-lit oil lamp with a voice like a kettle just off the boil, takes
your hands and says: "You carried our lost one home across the dark. There is a place at this
hearth for the lamp — and there is a place at this hearth for you. Stay. Be warm. You are one
of ours now."

Lumo, in the doorway, dabs at his shade with a small jointed hand and pretends he is only
adjusting his bulb.

Far away, on a green world, in the dark of one snuffed-out gym, a cold thing is still angry
about a light it could not keep out. It will press at the edges a while longer. But it will
never, ever find its way to a place like this — a whole world that is nothing but light, and
warmth, and lamps who leave a place set for the ones they love, however long it takes them to
come home.

You set down your pack by the homecoming flame. Flicker climbs to your shoulder, blazing,
home and not letting go of you either. The Brass Hollow shines. Somewhere a kettle sings.

And every lamp in Lampyria, that night, burns just a little brighter.
