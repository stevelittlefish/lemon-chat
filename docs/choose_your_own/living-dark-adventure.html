Adventure overview

You wake on cold stone in a vast underground structure built by a civilisation that has been gone — or changed — for millennia. You remember nothing. A small maintenance robot called Unit 7 is watching you. It says it can help you get out.

The Vel-Ahru Complex is still alive: its lichen breathes, its circuits hum, and something drifts through the enormous caverns that isn't quite animal and isn't quite anything else. The way out requires two components — but first, you need to know where to look.

Objectives

The Archive must be visited first — it holds the precise sub-location of each item. Both item areas are open from the start but the items aren't findable until the Archive records are read.

  1. Vel-Ahru Archive — south-east. Learn where both items actually are. Unit 7 knows the general directions but not the sub-locations within each vast area.
  2. Navigation Stone — Luminare Vaults, east. Challenge: Luminare communication encounter at The Plinth.
  3. Power Cell — Grub Warrens, north. Challenge: retrieve it from the sleeping Grub Nest.
  4. Activate the Ascent Shaft — north-west. Auto-succeeds when both items are in hand. No random check.

Special mechanics

Vel-Ahru Archive — the first required stop

A new required location south-east of the Awakening Chamber. Unit 7's briefing explains it knows the two items exist and their general directions (east / north), but the Archive holds the precise sub-location records it hasn't accessed recently.

Two state flags gate the items: stone_location_known and cell_location_known. Without visiting the Archive, the player can explore either area but won't know where within the vast space to look. This forces 3–5 Archive scenes before the fetch quests begin, and rewards exploration with specific knowledge rather than a waypoint marker.

Sub-zones within each major location

Each major area is a 3-scene journey, not a single room. The pacing rules enforce this: travel = one scene, arrival = one scene, encounter = a third scene. They cannot be combined.

  • Luminare Vaults: Entrance archway → Main Atrium (Luminares visible, healing herb here) → The Plinth (encounter)
  • Grub Warrens: Tunnel Entrance → Branching Chamber (three routes, alert check possible) → Nest (encounter)

Players must take at least one exploratory action in each area before any encounter resolves.

Grub alertness state

grub_alert_level (0–3) replaces the static difficulty table for the Grub Den. Noisy actions near the Warrens or a failed encounter raise it; spending a scene elsewhere lowers it. This makes every action in the Warrens area carry weight.

  • Level 0: sleeping — MEDIUM table
  • Level 1: one patrolling — MEDIUM, but partial costs 2 health instead of 1
  • Level 2: agitated — HARD table
  • Level 3: impassable — the tunnels cannot be entered; must wait one scene elsewhere (drops to level 2)
Unit 7 trust — maintenance task mechanic

Trust is no longer granted by vague politeness. It is earned through a specific, observable action. On the north-east wall of the Awakening Chamber there is a maintenance hatch Unit 7 cannot open alone — the mechanism needs two hands in a sequence its manipulators can't manage. Unit 7 keeps returning to it but has no word for asking.

The player discovers it by examining the north-east wall or asking Unit 7 what it keeps going back to. Helping resolves via random_chance — success or failure, the hatch opens eventually. Completing it sets unit7_trust = "helpful" and is the only way to earn it.

Unit 7 as companion

If unit7_trust = "helpful", the player can invite Unit 7 to accompany them (unit7_following = "true"). Unit 7 makes one factual observation per scene — never hints, never spoilers, just what it sees. It cannot enter the Grub tunnels (chassis too wide) or the Ascent Shaft (power grid risk).

At the Shaft base, if following, Unit 7 stops at the edge and delivers its farewell scene before the platform activates. It has had a very long time to prepare the words.

Memory fragments as keys

Memory fragments (0–3) still shift narration tone — but each one now also unlocks a specific interaction:

  • Fragment 1: Player can introduce themselves by name. Unit 7 uses their name from then on. Unlocks "who are you looking for?" dialogue.
  • Fragment 2: Unlocks "the signal" dialogue with Unit 7 (it recognises the frequency). On a return visit to the Vaults after retrieving the stone, the Luminares use the RECOGNITION table — a scene of being genuinely seen.
  • Fragment 3: Victory text gains a final closing paragraph. Narration becomes fully grounded.
Player journal

At adventure start, the GM creates c.journal with note_save and appends a brief entry after each significant event using note_append. The player can ask "show me my journal" at any time to get a second-person record of everything they have discovered. The journal also helps the GM track state across long sessions without calling state_list repeatedly.

World events

After each scene transition, the GM calls random_chance. On success, an atmospheric event from the events note fires — woven into the opening of the next scene. 15 events total: dimming lichen, something vast moving below, Grubs rearranging in sleep, a Luminare drifting through a corridor it had no reason to be in. No action required, no mechanical effect. The complex feels inhabited.

Why approach tables

The Luminare encounter has no fixed difficulty. The player's described approach determines which table is used — patient and open gets the easy list, aggressive or demanding gets the hard list. This makes the encounter feel like genuine communication: the mechanics reward the behaviour that fits the fiction.

Setup steps

  1. Go to Settings → Notes and create each of the seven notes below, pasting the content exactly.
  2. After saving each note, enable Read-only for every note marked read-only.
  3. Go to Settings → Characters, open Choose Your Own, and add note_save and note_append to its tools list if they are not already present.
  4. Use the existing Choose Your Own character. No new character is needed — it finds all g.cyo.* adventures automatically.
  5. Start a new conversation with Choose Your Own. It will offer The Living Dark alongside any other installed adventures.
Haven't set up Choose Your Own yet? See the Eldoria adventure guide for the full character setup and system prompt.
Easier for younger players: Before starting, edit g.cyo.living-dark and change player_health = "3" to "5". Turn read-only back on after editing.
Upgrading from v1? Import the note pack from note-packs/living-dark.json via Settings → Tools → Import note pack. All six existing notes are overwritten with v2 content and the new events note is created. Then add note_save and note_append to the character's tools.

Character

This adventure uses the existing Choose Your Own character. No new character is needed. The system prompt scans for all notes matching g.cyo.<name> and presents them as adventure options. Once g.cyo.living-dark exists, it appears in the list automatically.

For v2, two additional tools are required: note_save (to create the player journal) and note_append (to add journal entries). Add them to the character's tools list in Settings → Characters.

Starting play

  1. Start a new conversation with Choose Your Own.
  2. It will present available adventures. Say "The Living Dark".
  3. The GM loads lore, sets world state, creates the journal, generates an opening image, and reads the intro. Play begins.
  4. Each response ends with three choices. Pick one, or type something else.
The maintenance hatch is how you earn Unit 7's trust. Examine the north-east wall of the Awakening Chamber or ask Unit 7 what it keeps returning to. Helping opens it. Trust is mechanically rewarded — it grants advantage on the Grub Den and lets you invite Unit 7 to come with you.
Visit the Archive before the item areas. The Luminare Vaults and Grub Warrens are vast — without the Archive records, you can explore them but won't be able to pinpoint the items. Unit 7 knows the general directions (east / north) but not the sub-locations.
Ask to see your journal at any time. Type "show me my journal" and the GM will read it. It tracks what you have discovered. It also helps the GM orient if context is getting long.
If the GM declares an outcome before rolling: say "You need to record the outcomes before calling pick_random — check the rules note."

Adjusting difficulty

Turn off read-only, edit, then re-enable read-only.

  • Easier: In g.cyo.living-dark, change player_health = "3" to "5".
  • Easier: In g.cyo.living-dark.bestiary, change the Grub Den MEDIUM list to EASY.
  • Easier (skip archive gate): In g.cyo.living-dark, change both location flags to "true" in WORLD STATE INIT so the player always knows where the items are. This removes the Archive requirement and makes it a pure optional location.
  • Harder: Change starting health to "2".
  • Harder: Remove the healing herb entry from g.cyo.living-dark.rules.
  • Harder: Set grub_alert_level = "1" in WORLD STATE INIT — the Warrens start with one Grub already patrolling.
  • Much harder: In g.cyo.living-dark.world, remove the compass direction from each location description. The player must discover the layout themselves.
