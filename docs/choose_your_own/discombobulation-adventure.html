Adventure overview

You wake face-down on a cubicle partition at Discombobulators Inc. with a crumpled excerpt of SOP-442-B in your hand and a stapler that is inexplicably hot. The Phase IV report is overdue. The variance is at 11.3%. A man whose face is too symmetrical has mentioned a Personnel Purge. The building locks at 6:00 PM, and no clock in the building will ever show 6:00.

This is a corporate liminal nightmare: the office is too large for the building, every clock disagrees and all of them are correct, and every rule almost makes sense. The procedures are followed exactly. That is the problem. Get the report corrected, aligned, stamped, and submitted — then get through the revolving door before close of business. Leaving is technically a breach of contract. Leave anyway.

Objectives

The chain is strict: the Delta-logs must be corrected before the Alignment will run, the report must exist and compliance must be Acceptable before Vane's stamp can be obtained, and the Submission Portal only accepts a stamped report. The revolving door only turns once the report is submitted.

  1. Delta-logs — Records and Reconciliation, down the east stairwell. Pat must reconcile them.
  2. The Alignment — Cubicle 4-B terminal. Run Corrective Modulation until variance hits exactly 0.04%. Zero is prohibited.
  3. Vane's sign-off — third floor (the building has two storeys). Resolve the Urgent-yet-Low-Priority email chain to learn which VANE door is the corner office.
  4. Submission — the Recursive Hallway in the HR wing. No eye contact. The portal is a beige fax machine on a plinth.
  5. Escape — the revolving door, before the official clock passes 6:00 PM.

Special mechanics

The official clock and temporal recession

office_clock starts at 4:47 PM and advances 13 minutes per travel between areas — never for actions within an area. Wall clocks always show a slightly different time from the official clock and from each other. All of them are correct. This is never presented as strange.

Coffee causes localized temporal recession: the one coffee the vending machine will dispense recedes the clock 26 minutes (and restores 2 bandwidth). A break room detour costs two transitions — 26 minutes — so the coffee pays for the trip exactly. If the clock passes 6:00 PM, the lockdown begins: lights go off wing by wing and the player gets one final HARD-table transit to the lobby.

Compliance ladder and the promotion trap

corporate_compliance runs Noncompliant → Marginal → Acceptable → Exemplary. Official documents raise it; procedural violations (reply-all incidents) lower it. Director Vane's calendar does not admit anyone below Acceptable.

Reaching Exemplary triggers the Promotion: permanent on-site presence, a dedicated office with the player's jacket already in it. Declining requires Form 27-C, which must be requested from the man promoting you. He is glad you asked. He always has it with him. Accepting is a quiet game-over.

The displayed percentage next to the rating is chosen fresh each time, between 20 and 60, and never corresponds to the rating. The rating and the percentage are produced by different departments.

HR engagement level

hr_engagement_level (0–3) governs the Recursive Hallway. Enthusiasm, eye contact, failed attempts, and reply-all incidents raise it; a full scene elsewhere lowers it.

  • Level 0: heads down — MEDIUM table
  • Level 1: one associate circling back — MEDIUM, partial costs 2 bandwidth
  • Level 2: a stand-up meeting has formed — HARD table
  • Level 3: an all-hands is in progress — impassable; it ends as it began, without a clear owner
Pat and the Q3 box

The only trust mechanic. The Q3 box on the bottom shelf of Records does not fit, and Pat keeps looking at it. Pat does not ask. Helping (it goes in the way it came out — most things do) sets pat_trust = "helpful": Pat reconciles the Delta-logs personally with no search check, the photograph is found in the box, and Pat shares the hallway advice — "Count the doors. Do not count the people." — which grants advantage on the Recursive Hallway.

Personal effects as keys

Three of the player's own belongings are placed where they will be found. Each restores 1 bandwidth and unlocks something:

  • The mug (break room, drying rack) — a name that is almost the player's name. They can give their name; colleagues use it, spelled slightly differently each time it appears in writing.
  • The photograph (Records, inside the Q3 box) — a life outside, suddenly specific. Unlocks asking Pat how long the player has worked here. Pat answers.
  • The jacket (HR wing, the one pulled-out chair in the empty meeting room) — it fits. Unlocks the closing paragraph of the victory text.
The audit

The Compliance Officer — the symmetrical man — appears mid-aisle, at most twice per game, and asks three questions from SOP-442-B. Consulting the crumpled excerpt passes automatically. Failure applies a Corrective Modulation (1 bandwidth) and issues Form 11-D, Acknowledgement of Acknowledgement, already signed in the player's handwriting — which is an official document, so compliance rises. Either way, compliance rises. The audit cannot lower compliance. The audit is how compliance is made.

The SOP as an in-fiction prop

The full text of SOP-442-B is a note. When the player reads their crumpled excerpt, the GM quotes one subsection at a time, "according to current operational need." Audit questions come from it. Everywhere else, the GM cites real section numbers slightly mismatched to what they are citing, and never acknowledges the mismatch. Every employee treats the document as complete, sufficient, and clear.

World events

After each scene transition, the GM calls random_chance. On success, an ambient event fires: a phone answered on the second ring in an empty cubicle, a printer producing one warm blank page, the smell of birthday cake from no direction, a door closing with the sound of a door opening. 19 events in five groups. No action required, no mechanical effect, never explained, never repeated.

Why the rules almost make sense

Every individual rule is followable, and the GM-facing mechanics resolve cleanly — tables, flags, counters. It is the fiction the mechanics describe that does not quite close: a percentage produced by a different department than its rating, an audit that can only raise the score it audits, a variance that must never reach the ideal it measures. Nobody in the building notices, because in the building these are not contradictions. They are procedure.

Setup steps

  1. Go to Settings → Notes and create each of the eight notes below, pasting the content exactly. Or import the note pack from note-packs/discombobulation.json via Settings → Tools → Import note pack to create them all at once.
  2. After saving each note, enable Read-only for every note marked read-only.
  3. Go to Settings → Characters, open Choose Your Own, and confirm note_save and note_append are in its tools list (they are required for the activity log; The Living Dark v2 already added them).
  4. Use the existing Choose Your Own character. No new character is needed — it finds all g.cyo.* adventures automatically.
  5. Start a new conversation with Choose Your Own. It will offer Adventure in Discombobulation alongside any other installed adventures.
Haven't set up Choose Your Own yet? See the Eldoria adventure guide for the full character setup and system prompt.
Easier for a gentler shift: Before starting, edit g.cyo.discombobulation and change mental_bandwidth = "4" to "6". Turn read-only back on after editing.

Starting play

  1. Start a new conversation with Choose Your Own.
  2. It will present available adventures. Say "Adventure in Discombobulation".
  3. The GM loads lore, sets world state, creates the activity log, generates an opening image, and reads the intro. The clock says 4:47 PM. Play begins.
  4. Each response ends with the status line and three choices. One of the three will seem to have been misfiled from a different day. Pick one, or type something else.
Help Pat with the Q3 box. Examine the bottom shelf in Records, or ask Pat what they keep looking at. Helping gets the Delta-logs reconciled without a search, reveals the photograph, and earns the hallway advice — advantage on the Recursive Hallway.
The break room pays for itself. A detour there costs two clock advances (26 minutes), and the one coffee the vending machine will dispense recedes the clock exactly 26 minutes — plus 2 bandwidth, plus the mug is in the drying rack. The building never learned to schedule caffeine.
Keep the SOP excerpt in hand during audits. When the symmetrical man appears, consulting the crumpled excerpt passes the audit automatically. Answering from memory is a roll.
Do not be too compliant. At Exemplary compliance, you are promoted to a position requiring permanent on-site presence. Declining requires Form 27-C, which must be requested from the man promoting you. He is glad you asked.
If the GM declares an outcome before rolling: say "You need to record the outcomes before calling pick_random — check the rules note."

Adjusting difficulty

Turn off read-only, edit, then re-enable read-only.

  • Easier: In g.cyo.discombobulation, change mental_bandwidth = "4" to "6".
  • Easier: Change office_clock = "4:47 PM" to "4:21 PM" — two extra transitions before lockdown.
  • Easier: In g.cyo.discombobulation.rules, allow the coffee twice. The vending machine misreads the fiscal quarter.
  • Harder: Starting bandwidth "3", or start the clock at "5:00 PM".
  • Harder: Set hr_engagement_level = "1" in WORLD STATE INIT — one associate is already circling back.
  • Much harder: Remove the COFFEE RECESSION paragraph from the rules note. The clock only moves forward, the way clocks are believed to.
