Adventure overview

There is no ground — only the Cloudsea, an endless ocean of cloud, and thousands of islands floating above it. Grandmother Wren, the greatest sky-cartographer of her generation, has just handed over her skyship, her lantern (with something small and bright asleep inside), and her life's atlas. Every island in the sky is drawn in it, except one: the last page is blank, and it belongs to the Wandering Isle — the island that moves, hides, and picks who finds it.

Made for younger adventurers: huge exploration, weird friendly creatures, no game over, and a sky that pays attention when you are kind. Five strange islands can be visited in any order — geysers that launch whales, a village that hangs upside down, a forest of glass trees, a flying market that sells the wind, and a crater where a fallen star dreams auroras. Helping an island earns a petal for the ship's compass. Four petals wake it. Then it points at a part of the sky with nothing in it at all.

The five islands

  1. Fizzlemist Isle (south) — ticklish geysers, rainbow mist, and a froth-whale calf stuck in a bubble on top of the one geyser that never erupts.
  2. Tumbledown Isle (west) — hangs upside down; its waterfall falls up into a floating lake, and the village bell fell up into it too.
  3. Chimewood (east) — a forest of glass trees that plays one song at dusk, currently out of tune because the Bellbird's egg rolled into a dell where every echo comes back backwards.
  4. The Zephyr Bazaar (north, drifting) — a flying market where Madame Sirocco sells bottled winds and will trade her compass petal only for a wind she has never met.
  5. Emberfall Crater (far south-east) — always evening; a sleeping star dreams the auroras, and lately it is having a bad dream.

Special mechanics

Sealed island notes — spoiler-proof by construction

Each island is its own note, and the GM is instructed to load it only on first landing. Until the player sails somewhere, the GM genuinely does not have that island's contents in context — islands stay rumours and shapes on the horizon. This keeps surprises real and keeps the context small for local models.

No game over

The adventure cannot be lost. The ship has 3 hull; at 0 she crash-lands somewhere soft and somebody kind helps fix her — a scene, not a punishment. Failed checks bounce the player somewhere unexpected or make a creature laugh; they never close a path. The only ending is the good one.

The Rose — any four of five

The ship's compass lost its petals long ago. Each island's trouble, truly helped, earns one back. Any four of the five wake the Rose, so one island is always optional and the player's route is their own. When the Rose wakes, it points at empty sky — and the finale begins when the player follows it.

Lucky feathers — the player controls luck

Kindness that costs something, bravery on someone else's behalf, or a plan that deserves applause earns a lucky feather (max 3). After any roll lands badly, the player may spend one to improve the result a step: failure → partial → success. The dice belong to the weather; the feathers belong to the kid.

Approach picks the table, too: kind, curious, or clever approaches roll BREEZY; loud and grabby rolls STORMY. And a truly wonderful idea needs no roll at all — the rules instruct the GM to just say yes and make it glorious.

The wick — a nameable, evolving companion

Asleep in the ship's lantern is a small being of mist and light. Naming it is the player's first act. It evolves with the Rose: flicker → glow (1 petal — lights dark places, warms cold things) → beam (3 petals — scouts ahead, carries tiny things) → sunbloom (finale). Each evolution gets its own scene and image. What the wick actually is stays secret until the end.

Trinkets, odd corners, and bottled air

Poking around anywhere odd produces one trinket from a twelve-item pick_random table: a bottled giggle, a marble with a storm inside, a key for things that are not locked, an acorn from a tree that does not exist yet. Trinkets trade at the Bazaar and make excellent thank-you gifts.

Every island also has air worth bottling — a fizz-gust, an upward breeze, a chord, a star's sigh. One of them is the price of the Bazaar's petal, which quietly makes every island connect to every other.

Friends in the open sky

Captain Marigold — a rival cartographer with a green sloop crewed by eleven trained puffins — appears between islands to race, boast, and check in. The Grumble is a storm that has been lonely for a century because ships keep running away from it; befriending it is the kindest thing in the sky, and it remembers. Both pay off in the finale.

Images everywhere

The rules mandate an image at lift-off, at first sight of every island, at every island's Wonder (the whale fountain, the murmuration, the glass-forest chorus, the lantern ascension, the aurora moth-dance), at each wick evolution, at the Rose waking, and at the finale reveal — 16 or more across a full playthrough, in a vibrant painterly storybook style.

Setup steps

  1. Go to Settings → Notes and create each of the twelve notes below, pasting the content exactly. Or import the note pack from note-packs/cloudsea.json via Settings → Tools → Import note pack to create them all at once.
  2. After saving each note, enable Read-only for every note marked read-only.
  3. Go to Settings → Characters, open Choose Your Own, and confirm note_save and note_append are in its tools list (required for the atlas journal; The Living Dark v2 already added them).
  4. Use the existing Choose Your Own character. No new character is needed — it finds all g.cyo.* adventures automatically.
  5. Start a new conversation with Choose Your Own. It will offer The Cloudsea Atlas alongside any other installed adventures.
Haven't set up Choose Your Own yet? See the Eldoria adventure guide for the full character setup and system prompt.
Long sessions: this adventure is bigger than one sitting for most kids. The atlas journal (c.journal-style note c.atlas) records everything, so picking up a new conversation works: say "continue The Cloudsea Atlas" and ask the GM to read the atlas and state_list first.

Starting play

  1. Start a new conversation with Choose Your Own.
  2. It will present available adventures. Say "The Cloudsea Atlas".
  3. The GM loads lore, sets world state, creates the atlas journal, and opens at the lighthouse. The first decision in the whole adventure is naming the wick — encourage a great name.
  4. Sail anywhere. Each response ends with the status line, three choices, and "(or type anything — or sail anywhere)". The third choice is always the wild one.
Be kind to grumpy things. Everything in this sky can be befriended, every grump has a reason, and kindness literally picks the dice table. If a storm seems to be following the ship — do not run.
Bottle air everywhere. Every island has air worth bottling. One of the five compass petals can only be bought with "a wind she has never met" — so a bottle from anywhere is treasure at the Bazaar.
Feathers are for spending. Lucky feathers turn a bad roll one step better, and the choice always belongs to the player. Holding three feathers and never using them is the only mistake the luck system allows.
Ask to see the atlas any time. "Show me my atlas" reads back every page so far — and it is the save file: in a fresh conversation, "continue The Cloudsea Atlas" plus a read of the atlas and the world state picks up where things left off.
If the GM declares an outcome before rolling: say "You need to record the outcomes before calling pick_random — check the rules note."

Adjusting difficulty

Turn off read-only, edit, then re-enable read-only.

  • Easier: In g.cyo.cloudsea, change hull = "3" to "5".
  • Easier: In the manifest, change the wake condition from 4 petals to 3 (also update the rules note's Rose section and the status line hint).
  • Easier: Raise the feather cap from 3 to 5 in the rules note.
  • Harder (for a confident player): In the rules note, change CHOPPY to STORMY for bell-retrieval-style physical checks, and award feathers only for kindness, not cleverness.
  • Longer: Require all 5 petals. The Rose wakes at five, and every island gets its moment.
